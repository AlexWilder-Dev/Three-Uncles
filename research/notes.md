Read on 18 October 2026. Sources: every page on threeuncles.co.uk (home, /ourstory, /latest-news and eight news posts, /chinese-catering-london, and the seven location pages), the site CSS and font files, and the press and listings linked below. Raw page text is in `raw/`, press text in `raw/press/`. Images are in `images/` with descriptive names (resized to 1600px; the originals are at static.wixstatic.com, IDs in the photo log).

## The Brixton room and the market

- **Where:** Unit 19 & 20, Brixton Village, SW9 8PR. The covered market used to be Granville Arcade (their own beer post gives the address as "19 Granville Arcade"). The Good Food Guide places it "on the western edge of Brixton Village". Their own post says it is about five minutes from Brixton tube.
- **The unit:** this was the old Tiger and Pig site, 600 sq ft. When it opened, unit 19 held the seats and unit 20 the kitchen and counter (Brixton Buzz). Restaurant Online in 2022 said the dining area looked smaller than the kitchen, "with more chairs available outside in the market arcade than inside the restaurant itself".
- **The look:** a cobalt-blue painted shopfront with the THREE 三叔 UNCLES lockup on the fascia and a second fascia panel reading BRIXTON VILLAGE. There is red neon in the window, red benches and grey-topped tables out in the arcade, and bistro stools. Inside: "gleaming white tiles and throbbing red neon signs" (Evening Standard), an open kitchen, and roast meats hanging in the window (Restaurant Online). SquareMeal describes "tiny wooden stools at equally tiny tables".
- **The arcade:** yellow walls, strings of bulbs overhead, people eating at shared tables in the walkway (photo `brixton-shopfront-arcade-diners.jpg`). On a Saturday night the Standard reviewer walked in through "duelling soundsystems and crowds of stumbling day drinkers", and found Three Uncles "its own unflustered little island-state".
- **Service:** table service, eat in or take away, no reservations, first-come first-served (location page).
- **Opening date:** **March 2022**, confirmed. Their own Time Out news post says "opened with a bang in Brixton Village March 2022". Their "2nd anniversary" post is dated 12 March 2024. Hot Dinners announced 8 March 2022; The Nudge says it opened on 14 March 2022. The /ourstory timeline's "2021" is wrong.
- **Seats: not confirmed.** The page body says 35, the page meta says 30, and Brixton Buzz and Restaurant Online said 25 covers at opening. The site gives no Brixton seat count.

## Who comes when (Brixton)

- **Lunch:** queues. Time Out's writer had queued "around the block" at Liverpool Street before Brixton opened. A Tripadvisor reviewer reported a queue of 20+ at lunch, SquareMeal mentions lunchtime queues, and Palate says "there's always a queue". Eat in Brixton found that "people didn't have to wait long", and suggests a late lunch if you want to linger.
- **Who:** Hong Kong and wider Chinese diners, for whom it is food that "reminds them of home" (their own Ideal Magazine post). There are also first-timers: Uncle Sidney told Restaurant Online people "walk in and look at our menu and ask us to explain what our dishes are". The Standard describes families at lunch (an East Asian mum with her tween daughter) and passers-by checking the price on the window menu.
- **Evenings and weekends:** market crowds, especially Saturday night. It is open until 22:30 on Fridays and Saturdays. Monday is a short day (closes 18:30).
- **Cold weather:** people sit out in the arcade, and Eat in Brixton notes there are no blankets, so wear a coat.

## How they talk about themselves

- Plain and warm, first person plural, signed as a trio: "~Pui Sing, Cheong Yew & Mo (The Three Uncles)".
- Words they use: *siu mei*, *chopped to order*, *everyday food* / "everyday Hong Kongers", "make it available to all as you would find across the Far East", "out of Chinatown", "first-come first-served", "Queues from day one".
- Small jokes: "Small but mighty" (Camden), "Shop 'til you chop" (Brent Cross), the fire alarm going off every time they roasted the crispy pork in the first weeks (Ideal Magazine post).
- Honest about failure: their first shop, in Holloway, closed before it opened because of food hall licensing. They then signed a lease just off Bishopsgate (Deliveroo awards post).
- The method, always in the same words: "marinate on the inside, rub on the outside, hang and fan dry overnight, roast, rest, and serve". Elsewhere: a 48-hour marinade (Uncle Lim, Brixton Buzz), ovens shipped from Hong Kong, and about three days from start to plate (Restaurant Online 2022).
- On who they are: Uncle Sidney (Pui Sing Tsang) ran his own Chinese restaurants. Uncle Lim (Cheong Yew) and Uncle Mo (Mo Kwok) are chefs. Mo trained as a roast meat master, as his father had in 1970s and 80s Hong Kong. They want to be "a café within a community", "not a high street chain" (Sidney, Restaurant Online).
- Naming is inconsistent on their own site: "Cheong Yew" (/ourstory), "Chong Yew Lim" (Deliveroo post), "Chong Yew Lik" (Ideal post). I use **Cheong Yew (Uncle Lim)**, matching /ourstory and the trade press.

## What reviewers praise and complain about

Praise:
- The duck: "a wondrous blush pink" (Good Food Guide), "Fantastic roast duck" (Harden's), rose-pink and generous (Palate).
- Crispy pork skin, and the Hainan chicken ("profoundly, soul-soothingly chickeny", Telegraph; "the best chicken rice in London", a Harden's reader).
- The Brixton-only lai fun noodle soup made with duck-bone broth.
- Dim sum: lamb dumplings in black chilli vinegar, curry fishballs, mushroom & pak choi bao (Standard).
- Value ("very good value for money", Harden's), friendly and informed service (Palate, GFG), focus.

Complaints:
- Price against Chinatown expectations: "£10 for char siu?!" and "We could have just gone to Chinatown" (overheard, Standard).
- Accompaniments: plain rice and too little sauce. Palate wanted "another sauce for moisture and seasoning", and GFG says "the flavours could be ramped up a notch".
- Cramped seating and small stools. Queues.
- Few vegetarian options at opening (Brixton Buzz, Eat in Brixton). Since fixed: Salt & Pepper Broccoli (v) and Mushroom Mapo Tofu (v) are now Brixton specials.
- The odd dry or day-old duck (Tripadvisor snippets).

What this means for the site: explain the dishes simply for first-timers; make the £1 special sauces visible (they answer the "needs more sauce" complaint); show the veg options clearly; say the queue moves; show the three-day method so the price makes sense.

## Brand and type (from the site CSS and assets)

- **Fonts:** Pitch Sans Semibold (Klim) for headings and labels, and Rum Serif Light / Thin / Thin Italic for body. Both are licensed webfonts uploaded to Wix, so they are **not** reused here. Open stand-ins, chosen by rendering them side by side (`font-specimen.png`): **Red Hat Mono** for Pitch Sans (same monospaced, flat-sided grotesque feel; prices line up like a menu board), **Roboto Slab** for Rum Serif (low-contrast slab, similar width), and **Noto Sans HK** for the characters.
- **Colours in use:** logo blue `#3058A4` (wordmark, line icons, characters), cream `#F4E5DB` (menu strips, reversed logo), periwinkle `#ADBEE1` (their page background), red `#E8424C`, navy `#111D37`. From the photos: shopfront cobalt ≈ `#0754C3`, painted-red interiors ≈ `#B93725`, and the neon's hot red-orange.
- **Graphic devices:** triple hairline rules with a centred mark and a character at the end (烤 roast, 麵 noodles, 點心 dim sum, 喝 drink); duck / pig / chicken single-line icons; blue line-screen halftone on cream for old photos; vertical stacked neon THREE 三叔 UNCLES signs; white tiles with blue grout; blue-and-white rice-pattern plates; the 燒味 (siu mei) mark on packaging and posters; 见证 on press quotes; 豬 雞 鴨 as section marks on the old menu.

## Design reasoning

- **Palette:** cream paper and logo blue are the core, as on their menus and packaging. Cobalt is the shopfront paint, used for "fascia" bands that carry a site's name and status. Neon red-orange is used only for things that are live (open now, the selected sign, the total), as their neon is the thing you see lit from the street. Night navy is used only behind the signs, because neon only reads at night. No gold, no lanterns, and red only where their rooms use it.
- **Type:** a monospaced grotesque for names, prices and labels, set like their fascia and menu boards. A light slab serif for anything you read. Characters are section marks, never decoration.
- **Layout:** built from a shopfront rather than a web template. A fascia (which site, open or not), a window (what's good, with that site's prices), a row of hanging signs (pick a site), and a glass counter (build a plate). Menus use their triple-rule dividers. Old photos get the blue line-screen treatment they already use.
- **Phone first:** the first screen answers which site, open or not, and what's good, with directions and a call button in thumb reach.

## What the owners most want this site to do

1. Get walk-ins through the door of whichever site is nearest and open now, showing that site's real menu and prices. There are no bookings anywhere, so where and when is the whole job.
2. Turn office and event catering into direct enquiries on their own site, instead of a broken "Office catering" button and an external Tally form.
3. Show one group: seven years from one counter near Liverpool Street to seven kitchens, with awards and press, as the R200 "Most Scalable Concept" shortlist and the new counter-service model are about growth.
